## The real, actionable gaps (❌ items, grouped)

1. **Two named regions with real content but no landing page** — Nigeria (#99) and Canada (#100). Both have dedicated, well-developed blog posts, and both are explicitly named in the site's own "3-market regional benchmark" pillar content — but neither has a `/regions/<country>` page the way UAE does. This is the single highest-value gap on this list: real audience intent already proven (you wrote content about them), real infrastructure already exists (the region-page template), just not extended to these two.
2. **Platform-comparison content is entirely absent**: Discord (#80, #134) and generic "signal bot" positioning (#71) and copy-trading (#137) have zero coverage anywhere. Worth a deliberate call on whether these are worth targeting (Discord-vs-Telegram is a genuinely common comparison query in this niche) or a deliberate non-goal.
3. **Pricing-tier language gaps**: "cheap"/"affordable" (#57, #62) aren't targeted — consistent with the site's premium positioning, so likely intentional rather than an oversight, but worth confirming that's a deliberate choice and not an accident.
4. **A cluster of "how signal groups actually work in practice" questions** (#156, #158, #168, #167) — practical, credibility-building content that's genuinely absent, not just under-optimized.

## Notes on the 🟡 partial group

Most of the 42 partial items aren't bugs — they're topics genuinely *covered* somewhere in context but without a page built to capture that exact search phrasing as a primary target. That's a normal, expected state for a site this size (not every real query needs its own URL) — but it's also exactly the pool to draw from if/when there's appetite for more content, since the underlying expertise and FAQ answers already exist and mostly need to be extracted into their own targeted piece rather than written from scratch.

**This is coverage mapping, not a ranking guarantee.** A ✅ here means a page exists that
targets the query — it says nothing about whether that page actually ranks for it today.

ranking position over time (see the brand-visibility discussion from earlier this session).
